01 / Chiropractic
Specialist treatment

Chiropractic Care

The focus is on the musculoskeletal system, taking a whole-body view rather than focusing only on the painful area. Assessment may also consider metabolic factors and how the body’s systems work together. Hands-on chiropractic techniques can help improve joint movement and function. People enquire about migraines and neck-related headaches, neck and shoulder pain, arm and hand problems, and low-back, hip or knee pain.

Your first appointment

The first appointment lasts up to one hour and usually includes an assessment and treatment. Follow-up appointments are 20-30 minutes.

Podiatry & Foot Care

Other treatments

Back pain treatments

Whilst we treat all joints around the body, back pain is a very common presentation.

  • Spinal manipulation: Skilled, precise hand movements or controlled thrusts applied to restricted joints to restore proper alignment, improve range of motion and reduce nerve irritation.
  • Spinal mobilisation: Gentler, slower stretching and movement of the joints when high-velocity adjustments are not suitable.
  • Soft tissue therapy: Massage, myofascial release and trigger point therapy to ease muscle spasms and tension around the back.
  • Exercise and rehabilitation: Tailored stretches, core stabilisation and strength-building exercises to support the spine and help prevent future injury.
Neck pain treatments

Whilst we treat joints, ligaments and muscles around the body, neck pain is very frequently seen.

  • Spinal adjustments (manipulation): Controlled, hands-on force applied to restricted cervical joints to improve alignment, reduce muscle strain and ease joint inflammation.
  • Cervical mobilisation: Gentler, non-thrusting smooth stretches and side-to-side or figure-eight movements designed to loosen tight neck segments.
  • Cervical manual traction: Gentle pulling on the neck, often while lying down or sitting, to stretch the spine and relieve compressed nerves.
  • Trigger point and soft tissue therapy: Direct finger pressure or massage applied to tight muscle knots to release tension.
  • Instrument-assisted manipulation: A handheld mechanical tool applies a controlled force without manual thrusting; this may be used for older patients or those with joint degeneration.
  • Home exercises and ergonomics: Prescribed stretches, such as chin tucks or neck tilts, and practical advice on posture, pillow use and workstation setup.
Foot care solutions

Common treatments and services

  • Nail care: Treatment for ingrown toenails (including minor nail surgery or partial nail avulsion under local anaesthesia), fungal nail infections using specialised solutions, thickened nails and routine cutting.
  • Skin and lesion management: Mechanical debridement of hard skin, calluses and corns using a sterile scalpel, and cracked-heel (fissure) care.
  • Verruca and wart treatments: Specialised interventions such as verruca needling (Falknor's needling) and chemical therapies.
  • Biomechanical and gait assessments: Analysis of walking patterns, posture and lower-limb alignment, often paired with custom-made orthotics or shoe inserts to relieve conditions such as plantar fasciitis and Achilles tendinopathy.
  • Specialist health care: Diabetic foot evaluations (checking circulation and nerve sensation), arthritic foot management, and vascular or neurological foot assessments.

Common conditions such as heel pain, metatarsalgia, Morton's neuroma and plantar fasciitis are routinely managed.

Posture correction

Common examples of posture correction exercises for core stabilisation include:

  • Scapula squeezes: Squeeze your shoulder blades together and hold for 30 seconds.
  • Isometric pulls: Make a fist, extend your arms forward, then pull your elbows back while squeezing your shoulder blades for 10-second holds.
  • Wall angels: Stand with your back flat against a wall, feet slightly forward, and tuck your chin to align your head. Raise your arms into a goalpost position and slowly slide them up and down the wall without letting your lower back arch.
  • Chin tucks: Sitting or standing upright, gently pull your head straight back (like making a double chin) without tilting it up or down. Hold for 3 to 5 seconds to strengthen deep neck flexors and address forward head posture.
  • Y-T-W raises: Lie face-down on a mat or floor. Lift your hands slightly and move your arms into a "Y" shape, pull your elbows down into a "W" while squeezing your shoulder blades, then extend to a "T" to activate the upper and mid-back.
  • Gluteal bridges: Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat. Lift your hips toward the ceiling while squeezing your glutes and keeping a neutral spine to counter tight hip flexors and a weak posterior chain.
  • Doorway chest stretch: Stand in a doorway with your arms bent at 90 degrees and forearms against the frame. Step forward gently until you feel a stretch across your chest and front shoulders.
  • Cat-cow stretch: On your hands and knees, inhale as you drop your belly toward the floor and look slightly up (Cow), then exhale as you arch your spine toward the ceiling and tuck your chin (Cat).
  • Braces: A posture corrector brace is an accessory designed to pull your shoulders back and encourage a more upright upper spine. Use it only for short periods and alongside exercises.
Wellness and prevention
  • Physical activity: Aim for at least 120 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise per week, complemented by two days of strength, resistance and balance training. Brisk walking or short bursts of vigorous activity can help lower the risk of chronic conditions such as type 2 diabetes and heart disease.
  • Balanced nutrition: Prioritise a diet rich in whole foods, fruits, vegetables and lean proteins, while limiting ultra-processed foods and added sugars.
  • Sleep and stress management: Maintain a consistent sleep schedule to support immune function and mental clarity, and incorporate mindfulness, meditation or stress-reduction routines.
  • Avoidance of harmful habits: Refrain from smoking, manage or limit alcohol intake, and avoid substance abuse.
